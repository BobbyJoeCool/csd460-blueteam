Developer (Miguel)
All eleven steps pass. Two worth understanding rather than just ticking. Step 3 looks like a non-event but it is the point of the partial update: the servlet lowercases the email before building its own diff, so the page has to compare the same way or it would offer to save a change that writes nothing. Step 9 needed a fix during development — the confirmation panel was reading whichever option was selected at that moment for both sides of the arrow, so it showed "British Columbia → British Columbia"; the label each field held when the page loaded is now captured on load, which is also why Washington can still be named after the province list has replaced it.

One thing I did not fix, for the peer tester to be aware of rather than to raise as a failure: Step 9 names values in full and Step 10 reports them as codes, one right after the other. The panel is built in the browser from the dropdown labels; the summary is rendered by the servlet from the stored column values, which have no idea "WA" means Washington. Both are accurate and nothing is wrong, but a customer reading them back to back has to work out they are the same thing. Fixing it properly means the servlet putting display labels in the diff, which is back end work — logging it for Module 9 polish.

Also for the peer tester: after Step 9 the State field is genuinely blank until a province is chosen, and saving at that point gives "Choose a state or province." under the field. That is correct behaviour, not a bug.
Peer Tester (Sara)
Everything worked as expected. I think it was a really good call to put in summaries of the changes before and after they are finalized - this gives the user plenty of opportunities to review the changes they're making and confirm the changes after they've taken place. For steps 7 and 10, the popups did appear, but I was unable to grab a screenshot of them before they disappeared. This is expected behavior since these types of confirmation popups typically disappear after a short time.

Comments

Developer (Miguel)
All eleven steps pass. Steps 8 and 9 are what this test exists for, and the two screenshots are meant to be compared side by side: the reset endpoint is reachable without signing in, so if a registered address answered differently from an unregistered one, anyone could use it to work out which emails have accounts — the same reasoning the Login contract records for its single generic failure message.

This was a real bug in the first version of the back end, caught during review of PR #187: the password-format check ran after the account lookup, so sending any email with the public demo code 12345 and a deliberately weak password answered "New password does not meet the required rules" for a real account and "That code doesn't match" for an unknown one. Moving the format check above the lookup makes both answers identical, which is what Steps 8 and 9 confirm on screen.

Two notes for the peer tester. The test account is left with the password Newpass99! rather than the TestPass1! it was registered with — that is the expected end state of Step 10, not a mistake. And the site's password rule accepts only ! $ % * # as special characters, so @ is rejected; the checklist does spell the five out, but it catches people out.
